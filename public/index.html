WHAT YOU’RE LOOKING AT

A clear distinction
between position and picture.

01

The spacecraft is real.

Its position is calculated from CelesTrak orbital elements, updated as the clock advances. “Now” means a current orbital estimate, not direct spacecraft telemetry.

02

The Earth view is rendered.

Astronaut view fixes the camera at the estimated spacecraft position. It starts at the horizon in the direction of travel; drag to turn your head. This is a virtual observer, not the spacecraft’s instrument or actual attitude.

03

The photographs have capture dates.

NASA imagery arrives after processing. Terra and Aqua images correspond to those missions; other selected spacecraft use clearly labelled Terra context imagery. There is no universal live camera feed from arbitrary satellites.

ABOUT PARALLAX

Explore the view.
Know its source.

Parallax uses SGP4 propagation to estimate a spacecraft’s location from real orbital elements. Earth uses a NASA natural-color surface composite and optional dated Terra imagery. Sun direction controls day and night; an approximate Rayleigh model renders the atmospheric limb. There is no artificial night-side blue fill. Surface, clouds, exposure, and scattering remain approximations rather than current measured conditions.

The first-person camera stays at the spacecraft position. Its local frame follows the direction of travel over the rotating Earth. The default view aims toward the geometric horizon through a 62° vertical lens. Dragging changes gaze, not location. This is a hypothetical observer rather than the spacecraft’s actual attitude or instrument.

Positions are blocked beyond seven days from an element epoch. This freshness rule is not a positioning accuracy guarantee. Maneuvers and orbital errors affect predictions.

NASA MODIS images are dated daily composites. EPIC images are photographs from DSCOVR and show their actual capture times. Neither is represented as a live feed from your selected spacecraft.
