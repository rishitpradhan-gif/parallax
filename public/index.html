02 / READING THE SKY

A prediction
with a provenance.

Elements, then geometry.

CelesTrak’s public GP orbital elements describe a fitted orbit at an epoch. Satellite.js propagates those elements using SGP4. We transform the predicted position into Earth-fixed coordinates and calculate azimuth, elevation, and range from your location.

What this view can tell you.

A pass is a continuous interval above your selected elevation, sampled every 10 seconds. Each peak is refined around the highest sample with a bounded local search. This improves the numerical estimate; orbital and model errors still apply. Times are approximate, in UTC. Sampled intervals can miss brief grazing passes; boundaries are accurate only to the sampling interval, before orbital error. The 24-hour window can clip a pass at either end.

What requires another measurement.

Clouds, brightness, spacecraft attitude, sunlight on the spacecraft, local obstructions, and sensor pointing are not included. Geometric access does not mean an optical observation or image acquisition is possible. Orbital errors grow away from the element epoch, especially after maneuvers.

Calculations are restricted to within seven days of each element epoch. This engineering freshness rule is not an accuracy guarantee.
